Barrier evidence: keep sources separate

North · 40 surveyed homeowners

28 / 40 selected installer scheduling; 17 selected upfront cost; 9 selected unclear eligibility.

Multiple selections were allowed, so counts overlap. This describes North respondents—not a comparable overall ranking.

South · different evidence, not rankable against North

15 open cases reviewed (homeowners served overall not reported); 9 mentioned waiting for an installer. Other barriers were not systematically recorded.

Cases are not a compatible denominator for North’s multi-select survey.

Turnaround: recent figure, no valid comparison

8 days median among 11 South cases with a recorded time; 4 of 15 reviewed cases had no time recorded.

“Around two weeks last spring” is an unmethoded note with no identified cases, sample size, or method. It is not a comparable baseline: improvement cannot be inferred.

What’s needed to answer both parts

  • Shared reporting period and form; consistent population and denominators.
  • Barrier recording for all sources, plus a rule for multi-select/overlap.
  • Turnaround definition and complete, consistently collected times for a recent period and comparable baseline.

Bottom line: North’s leading recorded selection is installer scheduling; the most common barrier overall remains unknown. Service improvement is also undetermined.

Do not pool or compare partner counts as an overall ranking: North surveyed 40 homeowners with overlapping multi-select responses, while South reviewed 15 open cases with no served-population denominator and incomplete barrier recording. The South median is based on 11 recorded times, with four missing; the spring note is not methodologically comparable. A shared reporting specification and comparable baseline are required.
