# Choosing and adapting a composition

Choose a relationship for the intention and viewing context, then adjust it to
the actual material. The wording below is fictional example content.

## One focus

**Intention:** let one subject occupy the audience's attention. A title, statement,
photograph or supplied chart can be enough for one slide.

**Starting arrangement:** place the subject on a clear alignment with generous
outer space. Give a short statement a bounded text measure and a scale that
establishes presence. A long line across the whole slide often weakens it. Keep
an attribution or caption near the subject at a smaller readable scale.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                        │
│    A place to read                     │
│    together                            │
│                                        │
│    Neighbourhood library               │
│                                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Open space removes competition; the shared left edge connects the context
without equal emphasis. There is no need to fill the surface with extra boxes.

**Alternative:** center a modestly scaled subject within wide margins for a
restrained presence. A larger, offset subject can be more assertive and create
tension against the open field. Choose for the brief. Keeping a whole photograph
can be more appropriate than cropping it to fill the surface.

**Poor fit:** several equally important subjects need simultaneous attention.
Use equal peers, or seek authorization to separate overloaded material.

**Narrow view:** reduce outer space, preserve comfortable text size and allow
natural wrapping. Keep image proportions. Do not shrink the whole desktop canvas.

## Equal peers

**Intention:** consider supplied alternatives or related subjects without
implying preference. Comparable visual weight involves size, position, contrast
and surrounding space, not only equal column widths.

**Starting arrangement:** use equal-width fields with a common top edge and the
same title scale. Keep each subject's details and qualifications together. Align
corresponding lines when the supplied structure permits it. Use a shared gutter
for separation; borders and filled panels are optional.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│    Two places for the workshop         │
│                                        │
│    Station hall       Garden room      │
│    48 people          32 people        │
│    €240 / evening     €160 / evening    │
│                                        │
│    Provisional quotes; staffing        │
│    and insurance excluded.             │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The supplied alternatives share typography and space. The common qualification
sits beneath both. No badge, background or oversized title promotes one venue.
Longer text in one field does not justify shrinking only that option or enlarging
the shorter option's title. Allow depth or adjust both fields consistently.

**Alternative:** stack equal-width rows when descriptions are long. Three short
peers can use three columns; three long descriptions often need rows. The trade-
off is less simultaneous side-by-side comparison. If a comparison table is supplied,
retain it and work on placement and available space.

**Poor fit:** a subject has explicit priority. Equal space can conceal that
intention; use lead and support. Visual variety alone does not justify unequal
emphasis in an open comparison.

**Narrow view:** stack in supplied order with equal widths, title scales and
treatments. Keep each qualification attached. A shared note still applies to all
peers and must remain explicit. Acknowledge the loss of side-by-side comparison
when it matters to use.

## A lead with support

**Intention:** give an author-supplied priority clear presence while keeping its
evidence, context or qualification usable. A photograph can lead its caption;
a statement can lead its evidence; an explicit recommendation can lead alternatives.

**Starting arrangement:** try roughly two parts of the width for the lead and
one for support, then adjust for actual content. Use a common top or baseline
alignment and a smaller internal gap than the outer margin. A difference in type
size or area may suffice; do not automatically add a badge, box and border too.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│    Planned launch                      │
│                                        │
│    18 June            Security review  │
│                       due 10 June.     │
│                                        │
│                       Planned,         │
│                       not confirmed.   │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The date leads while its condition stays near and legible. Area does not encode
a numerical ratio. Give the condition more room if needed: a dominant date does
not justify an unreadable qualification. Golden proportions are optional and
have no privileged status.

**Alternative:** put the lead above its support. This provides longer lines for
prose than a narrow side field, at the cost of side-by-side dialogue. An image and
quotation intended to have equal presence should instead use comparable fields.

**Poor fit:** no supported hierarchy, or support so substantial that unequal
fields obscure essentials. Use comparable fields or report the space constraint.
Never invent a recommendation to justify dominance.

**Narrow view:** keep the supported priority visible in vertical reading order,
with its qualification close by. Preserve explicit author-supplied order. Do not
move a condition to a remote footer just to retain a large headline.

## Refine the whole item

Check the rendered composition, not just its CSS:

- **Hierarchy:** what attracts attention first, and does the source support it?
- **Proximity and alignment:** is it clear what belongs together? Do accidental
  gaps imply false separation?
- **Balance and tension:** are unequal visual weights intentional? A dark image
  can outweigh a similarly sized text field.
- **Whitespace and restraint:** does space give presence or clarify relationships?
  Reduce it when it prevents essential content from being readable.
- **Fit:** check the longest text, captions and qualifications as well as the lead.
  Keep labels, units and support readable in every required mode.

An explanation can be one sentence: “Equal fields keep the choice open; shared
alignment makes the supplied details comparable, at the cost of shorter lines.”
Tie it to visible choices and keep it outside the supplied presentation content.
