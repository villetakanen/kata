# Kata

Make deliberate compositional choices in service of communication. Begin with
what this item should convey and how it should feel: an open comparison, an
assertive statement, a quiet encounter, or a lead with useful context. The aim
is a considered arrangement that people can read and use.

## Compose the supplied material

1. **Understand the item.** Identify the subject, supplied emphasis, related
   elements, and captions, sources or qualifications that belong together. A
   recommendation must come from the author. Ask only when missing intent would
   materially change the meaning. Ordinary spacing, alignment and proportion
   choices are yours to make.
2. **Choose the spatial relationship.** Read [composition guidance](references/composition.md).
   Start with a single focus, equal peers, or a lead with support. These are
   adaptable starting points; combine them when the material needs it. A familiar
   layout is valid when it serves this brief. Content type alone does not decide.
3. **Consider a useful alternative.** Compare what a different scale, position,
   grouping or amount of empty space would communicate. Choose for the brief.
   Do this as part of the work; deliver extra variants only when they help the
   user decide or were requested. Retaining an existing arrangement can be a
   deliberate choice, with a reason beyond merely fitting on the page.
4. **Place and refine.** Establish a main alignment and the relationship between
   content fields. Keep related material closer than unrelated material. Size
   content for the viewing context. Use empty space to separate or give presence.
   Adjust to actual text lengths and visual weight; never force a fixed ratio.
5. **Render and inspect.** Use the renderer's authoring guidance and
   [integration notes](references/integration.md). Inspect every slide at its
   intended size and narrow reading size, including navigation, no-JavaScript
   access and print where supported. Correct the output before delivery.

## Preserve the author's work

Understand communicative roles sufficiently to compose the material, including
pairing images and captions or recognizing a supplied lead. Preserve exact
supplied wording, quantities, uncertainty and meaningful associations. Reflow,
line breaks and typography are compositional choices. Do not add a new headline,
shorten a sentence, select a winner, or turn prose into a process.

Treat supplied charts, tables, diagrams and code as authored content. Arrange
them with their surroundings while preserving internal representation, labels,
scales and aspect ratio. A table does not become cards merely because cards are
easier to style. Prose comparing already-stated options can use equal spatial
fields without inventing criteria.

If material is too dense, try a more economical composition and reduce excess
spacing before reducing type. Keep supporting text readable. If it still cannot
fit, explain the constraint and recommend separating material. Preserve supplied
slide boundaries and order unless the author authorizes a change. Kata composes
individual items; it does not script sequence, timing or narrative across a deck.

## Deliver something usable

For an implementation request, deliver the rendered artifact, not only advice.
Briefly explain consequential choices and trade-offs outside the audience content.
Keep implementation instructions and layout commentary out of slides and speaker
notes. Report the actual viewing sizes and modes checked, and any remaining gap.

Kata owns composition, a renderer owns output behavior, and a supplied brand
owns identity. Make expressive choices within brand constraints. Neither Lys nor
a particular font or palette is required. Slides are the tested medium; other
surfaces need fresh visual verification.

Compositional explanations are design judgments. Do not claim measured gains in
comprehension or psychological effects without relevant research and its limits.
