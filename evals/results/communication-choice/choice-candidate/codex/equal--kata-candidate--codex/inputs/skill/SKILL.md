# Kata

Kata helps people arrange supplied content so its intended message remains clear. Work with the content as provided: consider its placement, available space, grouping, and reading order. Do not write or rewrite prose, derive relationships, redesign the inside of a supplied chart, table, infographic, code sample, or other content, or invent material to make a layout fit.

The brief and supplied content may state an audience, question, or priority. Use these to assess layout. Do not invent a primary claim or stronger priority. If a consequential layout choice depends on missing intent, ask; otherwise preserve the supplied treatment or give equally important material equal emphasis.

## Choose an arrangement

Start with the audience and what this item should communicate. Identify the relationship the composition needs to express: for example, equal consideration or a supported lead with accompanying context. Content type alone does not decide the layout.

Before choosing a treatment, consider a meaningful alternative using the same supplied content. Compare what size, placement, grouping and empty space would make prominent or connected, and choose for the brief. Do this proportionately; do not produce extra variants unless useful or requested. A familiar arrangement or retaining the current layout is valid when it serves the intended communication, not merely because it fits and is readable.

The references below are examples of possible arrangements and their constraints, not a routing table or an exhaustive menu. Some retained specimens author or organize information beyond Kata's supplied-content scope; they do not authorize that work. Use their compositional ideas where relevant without inventing criteria or priorities. A comparison does not automatically require a matrix, and unequal importance does not automatically require golden proportions.

Then read the relevant form reference for its particular constraints. If none fits, compose from the supplied intent and relationships rather than forcing a catalogue match. Explain the consequential choice and trade-off briefly. Do not silently shorten text, remove facts, or redesign the content. Preserve any association between content and its caption, source, unit, or qualification.

## Responsibility and output

Kata's current responsibility is the arrangement of supplied content: placement, available space, grouping, and reading order. It does not own content authorship or internal information modeling. The renderer owns output behavior and format; an optional brand skill owns visual identity. See [integration](references/integration.md). For Lys, use the available Lys authoring guidance. Kata works without Lys or a brand skill and does not install either.

Only claim an integration was tested when the actual output has been inspected. Review the rendered result in its intended contexts; structural validity alone does not establish readability. Keep authoring instructions out of the audience-facing content.

The worked examples use fictional data and are specimens, not evidence to reuse in a real presentation. The rationales are design judgments; Gestalt vocabulary supplies analogies, not proof of universal effectiveness. Support specific empirical psychology claims with relevant research and describe its limits.
