The reading surface
is part of the work.

The shared intention is to let a reader connect a composition with its explanation and understand the accepted trade-offs. These three directions vary the emphasis within that intention. They are design hypotheses, not measured reader outcomes.

Each treatment uses the same heading, intention, complete commentary and frozen preview from “Let the image lead”. The palette around the specimen stays neutral. Type, measure, spacing, alignment and the relationship between image and notes vary together; this is a comparison of coherent treatments, not an experiment isolating a single variable.

The preview preserves the presentation composition even on a narrow screen. Its small text is not the phone reading version: the linked Lys specimen provides reflowed, selectable content. The surrounding commentary remains readable on this page.

What the current page reveals

  • The large specimen and plain underlined links are useful starting points. The commentary sits close to the work it explains.
  • Small uppercase annotation labels distinguish categories, but the body text spans broad columns. In this review, that makes the explanations feel more uniform than their roles warrant.
  • The green-grey surround, cream specimens, orange accents and shadows already imply a visual character. We have no recorded adoption rationale for that combination; it should be questioned rather than inherited.
  • The existing print stylesheet removes narration. A design study needs to keep both the work and the reasoning in print.

What this treatment tries. Georgia body text at 19px, a 58-character commentary measure, and generous vertical intervals give the prose a sustained cadence. Small sans-serif labels distinguish its roles. Whitespace separates arguments without enclosing each in a card.

What it gives up. The specimen is smaller and the page longer. The text can acquire more authority than an exploratory judgment deserves. On a phone, the single-column relationship stays largely intact.

What this treatment tries. System sans-serif text at 16px and a 1.45:1 specimen-to-notes split put the evidence and explanation alongside one another. A thin vertical rule marks the change of role; monospace labels give the annotations a working character.

Round 2.
A deliberate type choice.

Owner feedback · 15 October 2026

The owner connected Quiet editorial’s reflective character with Kata being thoughtful, and raised the possibility of serifs inviting slower reading. That is an expressive intention to explore, not an established effect on reading speed. Archivo had been the owner’s expected choice, as in many of their projects.

New constraints: F/OSS licensing, contemporary design, and an intentional choice for Kata. Georgia is rejected as a candidate; Times New Roman and Palatino are also excluded. The first round above that used Georgia remains a historical exploration, not an identity proposal. Quiet editorial is the direction under further investigation, not an adopted foundation.

Three families now carry the same specimen, commentary and longer vision excerpt. All use the same 680px maximum reading width, neutral colours and single-column structure. Sizes and line spacing are tuned per face to compare plausible reading settings rather than identical CSS numbers. Labels, captions, italics, bold and links also use the candidate family. The specimen image stays unchanged.

“Modern” here means a contemporary family deliberately considered for current documentation. It does not mean without historical influences: Archivo itself draws on earlier grotesques, and the serifs also have typographic ancestry.

Deliberate composition for communication

Deliberate composition for communication

Kata helps people and agents make deliberate compositional choices, beyond merely producing a readable arrangement. Its starting question is what should this presentation item communicate? This includes both intended understanding and expressive character: alternatives may deserve equal consideration; a composition may invite quiet reflection or demand attention.

The goal is to move beyond generic, mediocre defaults towards thought-out, sensible forms. Readability is necessary, but it does not by itself make a composition considered or appropriate. Kata should offer meaningful alternatives even when an existing arrangement is readable. Keeping that arrangement can still be a deliberate choice, supported by communicative intent.

Setting. 18px / 1.75 body; 42px / 1.12 heading; weight 400 for reading, 500 for headings, 600 for labels. Width axis stays at 100%. Captions are 14px and labels 15px; headings reduce on phones. Roman and genuine italic variable files are bundled locally.

Design hypothesis. A direct, even voice. In this setting I would watch whether the uniform texture makes longer explanations feel too much like interface copy, or instead keeps the reasoning pleasantly plain.

Design hypothesis. A firmer, more visibly articulated reading texture than Newsreader is the hypothesis to inspect. It could give Kata deliberateness without delicacy, but may feel too bookish or dense at this measure.

What to judge next

Which face makes Kata feel thoughtful without becoming precious, academic or nostalgic? Read the longer paragraphs as well as the heading. Then inspect the italic, caption and phone view: a pleasing title alone is insufficient.

Initial agent recommendation: Newsreader, because its difference from Archivo is clear without needing a more emphatic layout; Literata if that fluidity feels too delicate. The owner subsequently preferred Newsreader after reviewing its philosophy, connecting it with deliberate web reading and “slow media”. This records an expressive intention and owner judgment, not evidence that the font changes reading speed.

All three families are distributed under SIL Open Font License 1.1. The exact unmodified files, copyright notices and licenses are retained locally. Pinned source URLs and SHA-256 hashes identify the Google Fonts repository revision used. No network font service is required. Generic fallback is a loading contingency, not an accepted alternative; judge the comparison only after the bundled fonts have loaded.

Inspect the proposed type settings
Newsreader · prose
20px / 1.65 · weight 400 · optical sizing auto

Title: 46px at desktop, 35px on phones, weight 500. Commentary headings: 18px, weight 600. Prose captions: 16px / 1.5. The reading measure is at most 680px; it contracts naturally on phones.

Archivo · navigation and actions
14px / 1.5 · weight 500 · width 100%

Sentence case and underlines identify working links. Navigation wraps on phones; no hidden menu or script is required.

Chivo Mono · metadata
12px / 1.6 · weight 400

Reserved for the study identifier, date, photographic metadata and technical settings. It is never used for the explanation. Compact metadata can wrap instead of forcing horizontal scrolling.

Proposed balance. Newsreader is larger than the supporting faces and holds the continuous reading path. Archivo is limited to short actions; Chivo Mono uses normal weight so its repeated character widths do not dominate. Annotation headings and prose captions stay in Newsreader. Their sizes are slightly increased from the single-family test to give these reading roles more presence.

Trade-off. Three families add download weight and a more explicit distinction between reading, action and metadata. The sample earns that complexity only if the supporting roles remain quiet and useful. Its unmodified font files prioritise reproducible review over delivery optimisation.

Review status. The owner responded positively to this combined treatment and authorised moving to the next foundation on 15 October 2026. These settings are now the accepted starting point for documentation. Cross-browser, actual zoom and assistive-technology checks remain open; acceptance does not close those verification gaps.

Typography accepted.
Colour comes next.

Agent recommendation: use Quiet editorial as the first candidate for the explanatory document. Its narrower reading measure and single-column continuity suit the amount of reasoning already present. Working notebook deserves a second look if comparing a specimen with its notes matters more than continuous reading. Exhibition catalogue is the strongest candidate when the specimen should dominate the visit.

Owner decision · 15 October 2026: Newsreader is the voice for Kata’s documentation copy. The owner values its philosophy alongside Kata’s deliberate character and the idea of web “slow media”. Archivo and Chivo Mono remain supplementary defaults where sans-serif or monospace is required; neither is the default for prose.

Newsreader · reading voice
Headings, body copy, explanations, quotations and prose captions. This is the documentation voice; presentation specimens retain their own expressive freedom.
Archivo · supporting sans-serif
Navigation, controls, interface labels and other functional elements that need a sans-serif. It remains part of the system without taking over the reading copy.
Chivo Mono · technical notation
Code, identifiers, timestamps, numbering and compact technical values. A caption written as explanatory prose stays in Newsreader; a technical identifier may use Chivo Mono.

Reference and adaptation: the owner’s blog design system assigns Archivo to its primary voice and Chivo Mono to data. Kata carries over the supporting families while giving its reading voice to Newsreader. The blog’s industrial character, modular scale and layout rules are not adopted by this decision.

Adoption scope: the Round 3 documentation settings are accepted as the initial foundation; later refinements should retain their rationale. The three single-family comparisons above remain as the evidence considered; they are not the final three-family implementation. Round 3 bundles and renders Chivo Mono alongside Newsreader and Archivo. The owner accepted that combined treatment on 15 October 2026. Georgia, Times New Roman and Palatino are rejected; Literata remains a considered alternative that was not selected.

Next boundary: investigate colour roles in Study 02, using the accepted typography. Do not turn these three treatments into a universal composition rule or a fixed study template.
