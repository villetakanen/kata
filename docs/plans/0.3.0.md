# Kata 0.3.0 plan

Initial draft, 12 October 2026. Status: proposed; revised after scope review.
This document plans work, not capabilities already delivered.

## Goal and boundary

Help people and agents arrange supplied content, choose appropriate spatial
emphasis, and recognize when the existing layout can stay. Keep explanations
brief and preserve the content's meaning and qualifications.

The main focus area is a field-guide proof of concept: a Lys deck presenting
every current form and explaining on its slide where it works. It combines
dogfooding, a usable demonstration, and a repeatable review fixture. This adds
a concrete focus to the tasks below; it does not replace the agreed guidance,
verification, or scope-documentation work.

For this release, prose, videos, infographics, code, lists, and tables are
supplied content. Kata can consider their placement, available space, grouping,
and reading order. Creating or rewriting them, changing their internal
explanation, and deriving information relationships are outside scope.
For example, placing an infographic beside its supplied caption does not
require Kata to redesign the infographic.

Understanding enough content to avoid misleading emphasis remains necessary.
The audience's question and any stated priority inform layout; Kata should
not invent a primary claim, rewrite a headline, or infer missing content to
make a composition fit. Ask when the intended priority changes the layout;
otherwise retain the supplied treatment or use equal emphasis.

The six existing patterns remain, with general assessment guidance consolidated
below the choice framework rather than attached to one form. Their long-term
relationship to this boundary is unresolved. This release neither expands nor removes the catalogue.
[Out-of-scope work and research questions](out-of-scope.md) are recorded
separately, without a commitment to include them in Kata later.

## 1. Support retaining and arranging supplied content

Revise the choice framework in `skills/kata/SKILL.md` to make the initial
boundary explicit and allow a concise "keep the existing layout" outcome.
Supplied content need not acquire a Kata pattern to be a valid input.

Check available space, grouping, reading order, and the association between
content and its supplied caption, source, or qualification. Treat a supplied
table, code sample, or infographic as content to accommodate; do not rebuild
its internal structure as part of this task. If a layout cannot accommodate
it legibly, identify the constraint for the author instead of silently
shortening prose, removing facts, or redesigning the content.

Keep existing pattern specimens, but reconcile their scope statements and
the split guidance with the documentation task below. Do not add a content
taxonomy or a new deck-planning procedure. Describe retained material beyond
the current boundary explicitly rather than presenting it as a general
content-authoring responsibility.

Acceptance:

- An unchanged layout can be a successful result with a short rationale.
- Placement and reflow preserve supplied content, meaningful order, and the
  association of captions and qualifications.
- Guidance does not require internal redesign, prose rewriting, or invented
  content to produce a layout recommendation.

## 2. Clarify shared guidance for assessing layouts

Keep this a minor adjustment to general rules for understanding supplied
content and assessing its layout. An isolated usage concern is a reason to
clarify a rule, not evidence for a new pattern or a large feature.

Consolidate the relevant existing guidance in a short portable reference,
`skills/kata/references/layout-assessment.md`, linked from `SKILL.md`.
The skill remains the entry point; this reference holds checks that apply
across forms; individual form references explain their particular affordances,
trade-offs, and constraints. Avoid repeating general rules in every form.
This separation should remain useful if the catalogue grows to tens or
hundreds of forms, without building a new taxonomy or scoring system now.

The shared guidance should help a person or agent assess:

- What the supplied content says, what priority is supported by the brief,
  and what context or qualification is necessary to preserve its meaning.
- Whether size, placement, grouping, and reading order express that priority
  without inventing importance, certainty, or quantitative meaning.
- Whether captions, units, sources, and qualifications remain clearly
  associated with their content and readable in the intended medium.
- Whether the current arrangement already works, and what consequential
  ambiguity needs clarification before changing it.

Use a supplied number with a qualification as one small illustration of these
rules. Include an equal-priority contrast to show how unsupported emphasis
can mislead. Neither example requires golden cut, a two-field composition,
or new gallery slides. Understanding content for these checks does not mean
rewriting it or modeling its internal relationships.

Consolidate rather than duplicate relevant checks already in `SKILL.md` and
form references. Keep proportion-specific guidance in `golden-cut.md`; link
to the common assessment reference where needed. The detailed checklist has
one authoritative home within the portable skill.

Acceptance:

- The shared assessment guidance can be used with different forms, including
  forms not yet in the catalogue, without requiring golden cut.
- The number example demonstrates a general principle; it does not become a
  new format, a mandatory layout, or a separate feature claim.
- General rules are discoverable from the skill entry point and are not
  copied across every pattern reference.
- Checks preserve content meaning, supported priority, and readable context
  while allowing a concise "no layout change needed" result.

## 3. Verify the field-guide PoC

Use the shared assessment guidance from task 2 to review the actual deck.
Verification should answer whether the artifact demonstrates and explains the
current forms faithfully and remains usable in its intended reading contexts.
A baseline-versus-candidate model evaluation is not required for this release.

Check every form slide for:

- A visible demonstration of the named form, with supplied specimen content
  and meaningful relationships preserved.
- An on-slide explanation of where it works and a meaningful limitation,
  consistent with the corresponding skill reference.
- Legible text and specimen, supported emphasis, useful reading order, and
  captions and qualifications visibly associated with their content.

Extend the existing project checks for deterministic catalogue coverage,
stable and unique IDs, reference links, source-data integrity, and offline
packaging. Check that required explanation fields exist, while reviewing
whether their actual meaning and presentation are appropriate separately.
This fixture check is distinct from the deferred automation for detecting
missing entries in an agent's deck-review response.

Inspect all field-guide slides at presentation and phone sizes, with keyboard
navigation, no JavaScript, and print. Record the sizes, modes, and artifacts
reviewed, failures found, corrections made, and remaining gaps. Apply the same
required review to any existing gallery or deck layouts changed during the
work. Automated structural checks do not establish visual readability.

Keep a concise 0.3.0 review record with concrete ambiguities or contradictions
encountered while applying the skill. Use those observations for small guidance
fixes, and verify affected slides again after corrections. An authored field
guide is dogfooding evidence, not an independent measure of agent performance
or reader comprehension.

Acceptance: every current form is accounted for in both automated checks and
visual review; its demonstration and fit explanation agree with the reference.
Unresolved failures have an explicit disposition, and the review record names
any remaining verification gaps. No new model-run corpus, comparison matrix,
or review-only runner contract is a release dependency.

## 4. Focus area: field-guide PoC in Lys

Build a field guide that lets a reader see a form and understand where it
works on the same slide. Cover all six current forms: aligned comparison,
whole and parts, nested groups, directed dependencies, time series, and
asymmetric composition (golden cut). This demonstrates the existing catalogue;
it does not expand its semantics or settle the deferred scope questions.

Start with one slide per form. On each slide:

- Name the form and visibly demonstrate it with a small, faithful specimen.
- Explain the question or situation where it works.
- State one consequential limitation or poor-fit condition.
- Link to the corresponding portable skill reference for detail.

The form must be visible in use, not represented only by a title or a prose
list. Keep the explanation on that slide; essential fit guidance must not
exist only in speaker notes. Where the guide's explanatory material naturally
fits the form, use it directly. Otherwise use an existing supplied specimen
alongside the explanation rather than inventing relationships to make the
slide self-describing. Reuse current examples and their fictional source data
where suitable. Authoring documentation for Kata does not add general content
authorship to the delivered skill's responsibilities.

Use this one artifact in three ways:

- **Dogfooding:** the author applies Kata's guidance while arranging the guide,
  and records concrete ambiguities or contradictions encountered.
- **Demo:** a person can browse the deck to see the available forms and where
  they work, without opening another document for the basic explanation.
- **Fixture:** stable form/slide IDs and reproducible source allow checks for
  catalogue coverage, preserved specimen content, and rendered regressions.

Prefer a small generated deck, provisionally `examples/field-guide.html`,
using the existing build and Lys packaging. Keep the current before-and-after
specimens available; avoid rebuilding the gallery or the evaluation harness
around the PoC. A short introduction or index is optional. Stable form IDs and
links should support later growth without designing navigation for hundreds
of forms in this release.

Acceptance:

- Every current form has a discoverable slide with a visible specimen, fit
  explanation, limitation, and reference link.
- Demonstration and explanation remain readable together at presentation and
  phone sizes, in no-JavaScript reading and print; keyboard navigation works.
- The checked-in deck is reproducible, works offline, and has deterministic
  coverage and integrity checks integrated with the existing project checks.
- The review record identifies any guidance fixes found through dogfooding.
  A successful fixture review does not establish improved comprehension.

Task 3 verifies this artifact using the shared assessment guidance from task 2.
The guide is an authored demonstration, not independent evidence of general
skill quality.

Correct the stale ten-slide description in
`skills/kata/references/integration.md` against the actual specimen count
(currently 14). Check release-facing counts if examples change. Keep
`skills/kata/` portable when copied on its own.

## 5. Align project materials with the current scope

Make the supplied-content boundary the project's stated current scope, not
just a caveat in this plan. Scope can change through a later explicit decision;
that possibility should not weaken today's description or imply promised
expansion.

Review and amend these living materials together:

- `README.md`: introduction, capabilities, example requests, and ownership
  descriptions should explain layout around supplied content.
- `VISION.md`: distinguish the current responsibility from longer-term
  aspirations, and remove wording that currently promises content authorship
  or information modeling as part of Kata's remit.
- `AGENTS.md`: align project judgment and ownership guidance with that scope
  so development work does not silently reintroduce excluded responsibilities.
- `skills/kata/SKILL.md` and its references, especially `integration.md`:
  align triggers, authoring instructions, example requests, and layer ownership.
  The copied skill must communicate the boundary without repository documents.
- Gallery/deck introductory copy, the roadmap, and 0.3.0 release materials:
  use the same current scope and distinguish retained specimens from broader
  capability claims.

Audit existing instructions to derive headlines, restructure questions, or
choose internal content representations. Reconcile those instructions with
the boundary instead of merely adding a contradictory disclaimer. Retain
source-fidelity safeguards and accurately describe existing examples; no new
content taxonomy or pattern redesign is required by this documentation task.
Leave historical briefs, released plans, evaluation reports, and frozen skill
snapshots intact as records of their versions.

Acceptance:

- A reader of the README and an agent using only the copied skill reach the
  same understanding of Kata's current responsibilities and exclusions.
- Public claims, development instructions, and delivered guidance do not
  contradict the supplied-content boundary or promise deferred work.
- Any retained example beyond that boundary is clearly contextualized without
  rewriting the historical evidence or claiming its capability was removed.
- Future scope changes require updating the affected living materials in the
  same change; the present scope is revisable, not merely aspirational.

## Completion criteria

- The delivered guidance supports retaining existing arrangements and placing
  supplied content without requiring content authorship or internal redesign.
- Shared layout-assessment guidance applies across forms; its small examples
  preserve supplied priority and readable qualifications without prescribing
  golden cut or duplicating rules throughout the catalogue.
- The review record accounts for every form and required reading context,
  dogfooding findings, and verification gaps; unresolved failures have an
  explicit disposition before completion is claimed.
- The field-guide PoC demonstrates every current form and explains its fit
  on the same slide, with reproducible source and verified rendering.
- The skill remains self-contained and its references agree; the field guide
  is a repository artifact, not a required skill-installation dependency.
- README, vision, development guidance, delivered skill, and current example
  and release copy consistently state the same scope; historical records stay
  intact.
- `npm run check` passes. Rebuild after changes to example data, generator, or
  theme and include generated HTML.
- Changed gallery and deck layouts receive presentation, phone, keyboard,
  no-JavaScript, and print inspection. Record remaining verification gaps.
- Documentation describes only implemented and verified capabilities. Work
  in the out-of-scope record is not a release dependency.
