# Kata 0.4.0: composition for communication

Proposed implementation plan, 14 October 2026. The design direction is agreed;
this plan does not claim the skill or demos have already been realigned.
See [VISION.md](../../VISION.md).

## Outcome

Help people and agents make deliberate compositional choices beyond readability.
Begin with what one item should communicate, including intended understanding
and expressive character. Supply a small, usable repertoire and enough judgment
to discover alternatives, choose intentionally, and adapt a form.

The 0.3.0 field guide made the conceptual mismatch visible. Five catalogue
entries describe information representations; golden cut is the closest to the
intended compositional practice. Readability and preservation safeguards alone
do not counter generic defaults. This release addresses that mismatch rather
than adding more information types or more disclaimers around the old examples.

## 1. Establish concrete examples of the intended practice

Create a small Lys demonstration using the same supplied content in different
intentional compositions. Start with a photograph and quotation. Explore lead
and support versus equal presence, and quiet reflection versus direct emphasis.
These are working explorations, not a final taxonomy or universal prescriptions.
Add a second brief with three supplied proposals to test equal consideration
versus emphasis on an explicitly recommended option.

Freeze the supplied wording and assets within each comparison. Record the
intention for each variant, including any changed priority in the brief; do not
pretend variants serve identical intentions when they do not. Label fictional
material. Explain what the arrangement is intended to communicate, the choices
that support that intention, and a consequential trade-off. Keep explanatory
annotations distinguishable from specimen content.

Use whitespace, tension, balance, restraint, proximity, contrast, and dominance
where they serve the brief. Do not turn that vocabulary into a checklist each
slide must visibly satisfy. Do not rank variants as universally bad and good.
Presenting variants in a demo does not introduce sequencing into Kata's remit.

Review the demonstration with the project owner before treating its compositions
as the reference repertoire. The question is whether it expresses Kata's intended
practice, not merely whether it renders successfully. This is a design review
milestone, not a reader-comprehension study.

## 2. Rework the repertoire from communication outward

Use the reviewed examples to choose a small initial set of forms. Each reference
should explain:

- What the composition can communicate, including expressive character.
- Which supplied intent makes it a useful choice and when it is a poor fit.
- How its compositional choices contribute to that effect.
- A meaningful alternative using the same content and the trade-off between them.
- How it can adapt to available space while retaining its communicative intent.

Names and the exact number of forms remain to be decided through the examples.
Avoid organizing selection primarily by information type or block inventory.
Reassess golden cut too: a proportion must serve intent, not become a new default.

Remove the five information-representation patterns from the active selection
framework unless a clearly compositional reference can be developed from them.
Do not relabel chart creation as composition. Decide whether old specimens are
archived or retained as clearly separated historical artifacts; update navigation
and checks so historical examples no longer teach the active practice. Preserve
released evidence and frozen evaluation inputs.

## 3. Align the portable skill and assessment

Revise `skills/kata/SKILL.md`, shared assessment, integration guidance, and form
references together. The installed folder must explain the practice without
requiring this repository or its vision document.

Begin with intended understanding and expressive character. Ask when missing
intent materially changes the choice. Offer considered alternatives even when
an existing arrangement is readable; do not require gratuitous redesign or a
fixed number of alternatives for every request. Retention needs a communicative
reason, not only a successful overflow check.

Keep content authorship and internal representation outside scope. A process
may deserve an item of its own and room for a legend; Kata does not divide prose
into steps or author the legend. Pair supplied descriptions with their images.
Recommend separating competing material when useful without scripting sequence,
timing, or reveals. Preserve supplied facts, quantities, qualifications, and
associations. Defer sophisticated caveat-prominence diagnosis.

Kata owns compositional expression within brand constraints; Lys owns rendering.
Update example requests to invite communication-led alternatives. Audit the
whole portable folder for conflicting historical instructions instead of adding
another disclaimer over them.

## 4. Verify judgment and rendered artifacts

Review each new composition against its stated intention:

- Can a reviewer explain why this arrangement was chosen for this brief?
- Do alternatives differ meaningfully in what they communicate?
- Does the rationale describe visible choices rather than generic praise?
- Are meaning and content preserved without internal redesign?
- Does the result show a useful choice beyond an automatic card grid or fixed ratio?

A familiar arrangement is acceptable when justified; novelty, ornament, and
asymmetry are not quality measures. Record project-owner feedback and unresolved
design judgments separately from mechanical checks. Try the portable guidance
on one unfamiliar supplied-content brief to expose dependence on memorized
examples; document the result as a bounded trial, not proof of general quality.

Read the pinned Lys skill before demo authoring. Rebuild changed example sources
and commit generated HTML. Update structural checks for the new active repertoire,
source integrity, references, offline packaging, and historical separation rather
than retaining a mandatory six-form count.

Inspect every changed gallery/deck at presentation and phone sizes, with keyboard
navigation, no JavaScript, and print. Record sizes, modes, findings, corrections,
and gaps. Check whether adaptation preserves the intended communication as well
as legibility. Run `npm run check`.

## 5. Publish a consistent release

Align README, vision if needed, AGENTS, roadmap, the portable skill, gallery/deck
copy, and installation examples with the actual reviewed result. Write release
notes and a review record; update the version only when the release is ready.
Describe implemented capabilities and verification limits accurately. Do not
rewrite the initial brief, released plans, evaluation reports, or frozen skills
to make the historical development appear consistent.

## Completion criteria

- The project owner recognizes the reviewed demonstration as the intended Kata
  practice before it becomes the repertoire's reference.
- The active repertoire starts with communicative intention and includes both
  intended understanding and expressive character.
- At least two content briefs demonstrate meaningful compositional alternatives;
  comparisons preserve supplied content and disclose changed intentions.
- The portable skill supports deliberate choice beyond readability, stays within
  one item, and leaves internal content representation to its author.
- The old information-type catalogue no longer defines active selection; retained
  history is separated and linked accurately.
- Structural checks pass and required visual review has a recorded disposition
  for findings and gaps. No unverified claim of improved comprehension is made.
- Release-facing documentation agrees with delivered artifacts.

## Deferred and open

Deferred: temporal storytelling through 1.0.0; content authorship and internal
representation; sophisticated caveat-prominence assessment; a broader model
benchmark; runtime upgrades and integration packaging. Non-slide media remain
part of the conceptual scope, without a support claim until tested.

Resolve during implementation: final repertoire names and size, disposition of
historical demos, the unfamiliar trial brief, and any limits revealed by phone
adaptation. These decisions should follow the concrete compositions rather than
precede them with a comprehensive taxonomy.
