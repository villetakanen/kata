# Kata 0.2.0 plan

Agreed direction, 7 October 2026. Status: planned.

Make the five patterns useful when the information stops being tidy. Version 0.2.0 focuses on adaptation, perceptual review, and testing whether the skill improves unfamiliar material. Retain the original scope: slide patterns, with sequences only where the pattern unfolds over time.

## 1. Adapt the existing patterns

Add one difficult worked case for each form, showing when to adapt the layout or choose another representation.

| Pattern | Difficult case | What the example must demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparison | Missing values | Keep unknowns distinct from zero and avoid unsupported winners. |
| Composition | Tiny segments | Preserve proportions and make small parts identifiable without distorting their size. |
| Hierarchy | Shared membership | Recognise when a tree or nested groups cannot express the relationship faithfully. |
| Relationship | Dense dependencies | Use focused views or a matrix when a single diagram becomes hard to trace; retain the source relationships. |
| Change over time | Irregular or missing observations | Preserve time spacing and distinguish missing observations from measured continuity. |

Include plausible but confusing visual arrangements among the before examples, rather than relying only on prose-heavy slides. Explain the consequential choice in each transformation. Preserve figures, units, scope, uncertainty, and meaningful caveats.

## 2. Add a short perceptual review

Give the agent a compact review of intended grouping, accidental grouping, label association, emphasis, and reading order. Make the questions concrete:

- Do spacing, color, containers, and connectors suggest the same relationships?
- Is each label clearly associated with its data or object?
- Does emphasis help answer the audience question without implying an unsupported recommendation?
- Can the reader follow the intended order without reconstructing the source prose?

Distinguish live presentation from standalone reading when choosing density and supporting text. Keep research notes separate from everyday skill instructions. Gestalt psychology remains inspiration and a source of testable ideas, not a prescriptive doctrine.

## 3. Test transfer to unfamiliar material

Use the same unfamiliar briefs in three conditions: plain Lys, Kata 0.1.0 with Lys, and the 0.2.0 candidate with Lys. Hold the model, source material, output requirements, and brand constant; record the model and settings used. Include messy material beyond the five curated service-launch examples.

Compare data fidelity and whether readers can correctly answer the intended audience question. Record failures and ties as well as improvements. Separate the agent's review from actual reader feedback; attractive specimens alone do not establish improved comprehension. Use the findings to refine pattern selection before expanding the catalogue.

## 4. Demonstrate brand and temporal adaptation

Apply the actual Futurice brand skill to a small subset of examples, preserving the information's grouping, encoding, and legibility. Confirm the required brand resources are available before claiming the integration works.

Add one short sequence where the relationship unfolds over time. Keep positions, labels, and scales stable where their meaning is unchanged, and provide a complete static view for reading and print. General deck storytelling stays outside this release.

## Research boundaries

The Gemini report, “Slide Layout DeepResearch - Gemini 3.8 Flash,” in the Variety vault's Projects/Kata folder informed this plan. Its useful contributions are grouping conflicts, emphasis, label placement, and presentation context. Its prescriptions require qualification:

- Do not encode a fixed enclosure → proximity → similarity dominance hierarchy. Palmer explicitly states that common region does not always dominate other factors; competing strengths matter. See [Palmer, 1992, page 439](https://e-edu.nbu.bg/pluginfile.php/951829/mod_resource/content/1/Stephen-Palmer%20-%20Common%20region%20-%20A%20new%20principle%20of%20perceptual%20grouping%201992.pdf).
- Do not turn the redundancy principle into a blanket instruction to remove spoken text from slides. Mayer and Moreno describe conditions where concurrent narration and text helped when animation was absent. See [Mayer and Moreno, 2003, page 48](https://carpentries.github.io/instructor-training/files/papers/mayer-reduce-cognitive-load-2003.pdf).
- Keep prescribed 72-point titles, an 8-point grid, 60–30–10 color proportions, and a physiological justification for 16:9 out of Kata's core rules. The report does not establish these as universal requirements. Typography and palette belong with the brand; output geometry belongs with the renderer, while Kata checks that the result communicates faithfully.

## Completion checks

- Five difficult cases show adaptation or justified changes of representation.
- The skill includes the compact perceptual review and presentation-context guidance.
- A comparison of the three generation conditions records results and resulting changes.
- A real brand application and one temporal sequence have been inspected, including their static output.
- Existing integrity checks pass, with visual review of changed layouts, navigation, no-JavaScript reading, and print output.

The release remains a small repertoire with stronger judgment. A larger pattern catalogue, general narrative planning, and a separate kata-lys package are outside this plan.
