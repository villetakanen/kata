Proposed roadmap, 15 October 2026. The direction comes from the project-owner
review of the [communication studies](../../examples/communication/index.html)
and the subsequent discussion of fundamentals. This document plans work; it
does not claim a release or adopted visual identity.

explicitly waived further spacing/alignment/supporting-treatment studies: the
accepted typography, site palette, paper family and example colour roles are

## Purpose

Establish a practice of doing design through single-page studies that show and
explain choices, then publish those pages as historical documents. Use that
practice to develop Kata's visual foundations and an extremely light project
identity. Assemble adopted decisions into a living design-system page.

The existing communication studies demonstrate a useful direction: concrete
alternatives and narration make design discussable. They do not establish that
the current visual styling is intentional or worth retaining. Treat the present
palette, typography, spacing, and decorative treatments as unexamined defaults,
not an identity to preserve. Distinctiveness should emerge from considered
choices, not novelty or avoidance of a particular model's aesthetic alone.

This release focuses on visual fundamentals rather than adding layouts or a
larger compositional catalogue. It continues the purpose in [VISION.md](../../VISION.md):
help humans and agents make deliberate choices in service of communication.

## 1. Establish the design-study practice through actual work

A study is one browsable page that combines visible exploration and explanation.
A reader should understand the question, alternatives, and decision without
recovering a chat or opening a collection of implementation files. Keep the
format proportionate to the question; it is a practice, not a mandatory report
of fixed length.

Each study should make the following discoverable:

- **Question and intention:** what needs resolving and what should come across.
- **Provenance:** supplied material, references, constraints, earlier decisions,
  asset and font sources and usage terms where relevant. Distinguish borrowed
  references from original work and illustrative material from real evidence.
- **Exploration:** meaningful alternatives shown in context, with narration tied
  to visible choices. Explain what varies and what stays consistent.
- **Decision:** what was chosen, why, accepted trade-offs, and relevant rejected
  alternatives. Where no choice has been made, say so rather than inventing one.
- **Record:** date, contributors and their roles, review or adoption status,
  unresolved questions, and links to predecessor or successor studies.

Record meaningful human and agent contributions without turning the study into
a transcript. Separate intended effect from observed reaction and from the final
design judgment. A persuasive explanation alone does not establish that the
specimen achieves its intention. Retain disagreements or revisions when they
explain the decision. Do not claim reader outcomes without appropriate evidence.

Use the first foundations study to refine this format before creating reusable
authoring guidance. Keep any development procedure in a task-named internal
skill under `.agents/skills/`, following AGENTS.md; do not add repository publishing
machinery to the portable Kata skill.

## 2. Investigate a small set of visual foundations

Start by inspecting the current study page as a whole: which treatments are
accidental, what competes with the specimens, and what makes the explanation
harder or easier to follow? Use actual headings, body text, captions, annotations,
links, and specimens as test material.

Explore these foundations in a small number of coherent studies; do not require
one document per token or an exhaustive design-system inventory:

| Foundation | Questions the study should resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Typography | What voice and hierarchy suit Kata? How do reading text, specimen labels, and commentary relate? What families, weights, measure, and line spacing support those roles? |
| Colour and contrast | What roles need colour? How can a restrained palette support emphasis, navigation, and separation without tinting every specimen with a fixed mood? |
| Spacing and alignment | What creates rhythm, association, separation, and useful whitespace across the page? How should this adapt to available space? |
| Supporting treatments | Which rules, surfaces, captions, and link treatments earn their place, and which are unnecessary decoration? |

Study foundations together where their effects interact. Include realistic
content lengths and narrow-screen examples. Compare alternatives for a shared
intention as well as alternatives for different intentions when useful. Make
choices based on their communicative role, practical constraints, and reviewed
appearance, not merely the availability of a font or a familiar palette.

The project identity should be extremely light but recognisably considered.
Its expressive character remains to be explored; no particular font, palette,
logo redesign, or “studio” aesthetic is preselected by this roadmap.

## 3. Publish studies as historical documents

Provide stable, discoverable pages in the repository's published documentation
surface, with an index, dates, and clear status. Select the exact paths and
publishing integration during implementation after inspecting existing hosting.
Keep source and required assets available; links should survive later revisions.

Published decisions preserve what was considered and chosen at that time. Later
changes produce a successor study, with reciprocal supersession links. Do not
silently restyle old specimens with the current design-system values or rewrite
old reasoning to match a new choice. Preserve their appearance through scoped
or versioned styles and assets. Clearly note corrections to historical pages.
An exploratory page may be published before a decision, provided its status is
explicit; adoption is recorded when the owner makes the choice.

The explanation is part of the artifact. Provide a usable reading and print
view that retains both specimens and narration. A separate specimen-only export
can coexist with it. Where a study contains presentation demos, continue to use
Lys and the pinned authoring guidance; the study document itself need not be
forced into a slide deck.

## 4. Build the living design-system page from adopted decisions

The DS page describes the foundations currently used by Kata's project pages.
Show typography, colour roles, spacing, and supporting treatments working
together in real examples, not only isolated swatches or token tables. Give
brief usage guidance and link each adopted foundation to the study that explains
its provenance and trade-offs.

Connect these examples to the actual shared styles or tokens used by current
project pages. Apply the adopted foundation first to the study/documentation
surroundings and verify it there. Avoid creating a second demonstration theme
that diverges from implementation.

Keep the responsibilities distinct:

- Historical studies record how decisions were reached, including superseded
  choices and remaining uncertainty.
- The living DS shows what is in force now and where it came from.
- Kata's general guidance concerns composition across identities and media.
  Project-specific colours, fonts, and spacing are not universal Kata rules.

The project identity frames navigation and narration without constraining the
expressive range of specimens. A study may need a different visual treatment
inside its examples. Treat that as deliberate exploration rather than forcing
all examples to look like the surrounding site.

## 5. Review and verify

Review foundations in context with the project owner, and record the actual
choices and unresolved questions. Do not treat approval of the study practice
or the 0.4.0 direction as blanket acceptance of a particular visual design.

Check adopted colour roles for appropriate text and interface contrast, verify
font availability and fallbacks, and inspect typography and spacing with real
content. Check keyboard focus, narrow-screen reading, zoom, no-JavaScript access,
and print with narration. For changed presentation demos, also perform the
presentation and phone, keyboard, no-JavaScript, and print reviews required by
AGENTS.md. Record coverage, findings, corrections, and verification gaps.

Keep structural verification proportionate: working study and provenance links,
status and supersession references, included assets, consistency between live
DS examples and shared foundations, and preservation of historical specimens.
Automated checks cannot establish expressive quality or owner adoption.
Rebuild changed generated artifacts, include outputs, and run `npm run check`.

## Sequence and completion

1. Audit current defaults and choose the first bounded foundations question.
2. Produce and review a single-page study with real alternatives and provenance.
3. Refine the study practice from that work; complete the other foundation
   decisions needed for the lightweight identity.
4. Publish the historical studies and assemble adopted foundations into the
   living DS. Apply them to the current project documentation surface.
5. Verify, record review outcomes, and update release-facing documentation.

The release is complete when:

- Published studies show and explain the typography, colour, and spacing decisions
  needed for the initial identity, with supporting treatments addressed as needed.
- A reader can trace adopted choices to their sources, alternatives, rationale,
  date, and review status without the chat history.
- A living DS demonstrates the current implemented foundations and links back to
  those studies; historical specimens retain the appearance under discussion.
- Current documentation uses the adopted foundations while specimen expression
  remains independent of the surrounding project identity.
- Reusable study guidance reflects the exercised practice rather than a speculative
  template, and publication/status/supersession conventions are documented.
- Required checks and visual reviews are recorded with remaining limitations.

## Release bookkeeping and exclusions

The owner revised 0.4.0 to deliver foundations while explicitly deferring the
catalogue and portable-skill alignment. It is released as
[v0.4.0](https://github.com/villetakanen/kata/releases/tag/v0.4.0) on GitHub,
without website deployment. This site work must represent the
catalogue's provisional status honestly, without silently declaring alignment
complete or turning it back into a 0.4.0 prerequisite.

Excluded from this focus: a larger layout catalogue, a comprehensive component
library, heavy branding, content authorship, temporal storytelling, renderer
upgrades, and a broad model benchmark. The unit of Kata's compositional practice
remains one presentation item through 1.0.0; narration across a study document
explains the design work and does not expand that product boundary.
